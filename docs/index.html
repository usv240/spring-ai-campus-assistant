1 · How an LLM thinks

Guess the next word

An LLM looks at your text and gives every possible next word a chance. Temperature decides how adventurous it is when it picks one.

0.1 · safe, predictable2.0 · wild

Your turn: set the temperature to 0.1 and ask 20 times. Then try 2.0. Which one would you use to generate JSON for your code?

The chances here are made up to show the idea. A real model scores thousands of words.

2 · What you pay for

Text gets cut into tokens

Models don't read words, they read tokens: short pieces of text. Common words are one token, long or rare words get split. You pay for every token you send and every token you get back.

0words
0tokens (rough)

Your turn: paste a paragraph from your notes. Then try the same sentence in another language you know. Does the count change?

This is a simple approximation. Real tokenizers learn their pieces from data, so exact counts differ, but the rule of thumb holds: one token is about three quarters of a word in English.

An open-book exam for the AI

      
The model has never read our college handbook. So before it answers, we find the pieces of the handbook that match the question and paste them into the prompt.

    

    

      
      

        
        
      

      

      

      

        
        
      

      

      
Your turn: ask “Can I borrow a sensor kit for two weeks?” Which piece of the handbook comes out on top? Now ask something the handbook doesn’t cover, like the canteen menu.

      
Real RAG compares meaning using embeddings, so “presence” would still match “attendance”. This toy matches words, with a few synonyms, so you can see how the ranking works.
